# Screening — One Line, One Laugh (u-coral-404-one-line-one-laugh-22f2c9f3)

Judge criteria, in order: (1) hilarious on first read, (2) Surprise — the turn IS the joke,
(3) Economy — every word earns its place, (4) Freshness — original angle, no stock joke,
(5) Clean wording — grammar, spelling, rhythm, reads smoothly aloud.
Scores are 1–5 per criterion, in that order. First draft discarded per the writing-jokes
skill (the instant joke is the memorized one); these 9 were drafted across different
lenses (self-deprecation, absurd, observational, benign violation) and scored after.

## Why each non-winner loses

- **C1 (20):** "even it" is a good turn, but the punch lands on "password" — a flat final
  word — and password-manager jokes are a known family. Freshness caps it.
- **C3 (21):** strong turn (whispering "regret"), but "a man now stands by my bed" is a
  wordy runway for the punch, and the image needs a beat to parse — first-read laugh
  is weaker than C2's.
- **C4 (18):** a smile, not a laugh; the documentary-accent turn is mild and the
  inner-voice premise is familiar.
- **C5 (23, runner-up):** the sharpest snap in the set — snack request answered with
  therapy, 61 chars, nothing wasted. Loses to C2 only on criterion 1: it is clever and
  surprising, but the laugh is a knowing nod; C2's image (sitting on the floor next to
  the desk you bought to stand at) is funnier on first read and stranger.
- **C6 (18):** "union card" is cute, but Roomba-as-worker is a known gag family and the
  turn is telegraphed once "three days under the couch" sets up a labor frame.
- **C7 (17):** long runway ("looked at me like") for a mild turn; grill-as-person is familiar.
- **C8 (16):** pet-divorce is a stock joke shape; "citing irreconcilable differences" is
  wordy legalese that eats economy.
- **C9 (19):** "the cactus is smug" is the best single image after C2, but the first clause
  is a long runway and judging-plants is a known trope.

## Winner: C2

> I bought a standing desk and now I just sit on the floor next to it, out of spite.

- **(1) Hilarious first read:** the image lands instantly — you paid for the desk, then
  defeated it by sitting on the floor beside it.
- **(2) Surprise:** setup points at healthy standing; the turn yanks to floor-sitting,
  and "out of spite" reframes the whole act as petty rebellion against an inanimate desk.
- **(3) Economy:** 82 chars, no throat-clearing, no explanation; the punch word "spite"
  is the last word.
- **(4) Freshness:** standing-desk-as-wellness-purchase vs. floor-sitting spite is a
  collision nobody else in the field is likely to take; no stock joke, no pun, no saying.
- **(5) Clean wording:** plain grammar, reads smoothly aloud in one breath.
